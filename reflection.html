Reflective essay

How the five projects under Artifacts, my service roles at UMD, and this semester's rhetorical goals fit into a single story for employers, collaborators, and future advisors.

Throughout my time at the University of Maryland, my transition from student to researcher has been driven by a deep interest in computer architecture and a dedication to pushing the boundaries of the field. As I prepare to graduate with my B.S. in Computer Engineering and begin my Ph.D. as a Clark Doctoral Fellow, this portfolio summarizes the growth of my technical proficiency.

My engineering experience and philosophy are best reflected in my work on a 6-bit Dadda multiplier and an out-of-order Tomasulo-based CPU. The former required deep research into VLSI optimizations and architectural trade-offs; for instance, while a carry-lookahead adder is conventionally thought of as faster, my project demonstrated that a ripple-carry adder actually resulted in a faster and more power-efficient circuit in that specific context. The CPU project reflects my ability to translate complex concepts, such as out-of-order scheduling, into a complete and well-verified product. I guided the CPU from initial specification, using official RISC-V documentation, to a final GDSII layout, preparing the design for fabrication and tape-out.

My roles as a Technical Coordinator at Terrapin Works and as an Undergraduate Teaching Fellow have instilled in me a sense of professional responsibility and a drive for academic excellence. Managing end-to-end PCB fabrication deadlines and maintenance, as well as guiding peers through circuit theory, has taught me how to articulate complex technical skills and mentor others effectively.

In these positions, I have learned to navigate the inherent power and privilege that come with managing critical lab infrastructure and influencing the academic success of my peers. I recognize that providing equitable access to fabrication tools and clear, register-level technical guidance is essential for fostering an inclusive engineering environment.

Throughout my time at UMD, I have been profoundly supported by my family. Their significant financial and personal sacrifices made it possible for me to pursue my education. Inspired by their dedication, I maintained a rigorous work ethic, balancing three jobs during the academic year and working overtime during summers. This commitment allowed me to graduate in just three years while remaining financially independent. I plan to carry this same drive and resilience into my future endeavors, just as I have throughout my time here.

Looking forward, I intend to further my education by pursuing a Ph.D. in Computer Engineering, specializing in hardware security. This academic focus directly complements my upcoming ASIC Floorplanning internship at NVIDIA. The challenges I have navigated during my undergraduate career have prepared me to solve the complex problems inherent in the future of high-performance computing.
